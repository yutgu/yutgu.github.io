Hello, welcome :)

I am a fifth-year PhD candidate working with Xin Xie in the Department of Language Science at the University of California, Irvine.

My research asks how linguistic experience shapes the plasticity of human speech and language processing. As listeners, we constantly encounter variation — regional dialects, nonnative accents, unfamiliar voices — and yet we adapt, often within minutes. What kind of learning makes this possible, and how does the structure of a listener’s experience drive it?

I pursue this question along two converging lines. First, as the core of my dissertation, I develop new tools to quantify real-world linguistic experience and test how the type, amount, and timing of exposure shape adaptive speech perception. Second, I use controlled experiments to investigate the perceptual mechanisms behind adaptation, asking whether accent exposure reshapes how listeners categorize speech sounds or simply changes their willingness to accept unusual pronunciations. I also investigate how cognitive load and attention constrain speech processing when listeners encounter accented speech.
